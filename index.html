Nobody knows, and anyone who tells you otherwise is guessing. For context, 1,000 ng/dL is 34.7 nmol/L — above the top of the standard adult reference range, not in the middle of it. No. You order it yourself so the results belong to you rather than to us, and so you can keep testing long after we stop working together. We tell you exactly what to order and how to time it — morning, fasted, and repeated — because a badly timed panel is worse than none.

What if I am already on TRT?

Then this is not for you, and we will not take the booking. We do not prescribe or adjust hormone therapy, so we would be a second opinion without any of your history. Your prescriber is the right person.

Because that is roughly how long the hormone side takes to show up on a panel, and retesting sooner tells you very little. The pelvic floor work usually shows up much earlier, around week four, which is deliberate — it is what carries you through the stretch where nothing measurable has happened yet.

What if I do not have time for this?

Then save your money. The changes that move testosterone are sleep, body composition and alcohol, and none of them happen in the margins of a week you are not willing to change. We would rather tell you that now than take A$1,497 and watch it not work.

The panel

What to measure, and why each one is on the list.

You order this yourself, from a laboratory, and the results come back to you. Here is what every marker is for — including the three that most people leave off and then cannot interpret anything without.

Before you book the draw

Go between 7am and 10am, fasting. Stop biotin and any hair, skin and nail supplement 72 hours beforehand. Do not test while unwell or within 48 hours of heavy training. Expect to repeat it on a second morning before drawing any conclusion — a single reading is not one.

Total testosterone

Ask for LC-MS/MS

The headline number, and the least useful one on its own. Ask for mass spectrometry rather than immunoassay — immunoassay is unreliable at exactly the low end you would be trying to measure.

What it does not show:

Harmonised adult male reference range roughly 9.2–31.8 nmol/L (264–916 ng/dL).

SHBG and albumin

Essential

Most of your testosterone is bound to these and unavailable to tissue. Without them you cannot calculate free testosterone, which is why a total-only result explains so little. Rising SHBG with age can leave total looking fine while free drops.

What it does not show:

LH and FSH

The triage pair

The most important markers on the panel and the most commonly omitted. Low testosterone with low or normal LH points upstream to the brain — often reversible. Low testosterone with high LH points to the testes, which is not something coaching fixes and needs a specialist.

What it does not show:

Oestradiol

Sensitive assay

Testosterone converts to oestradiol via aromatase, largely in fat tissue. Useful context for symptoms and for understanding why body composition matters so much here.

What it does not show:

Prolactin

Safety

Elevated prolactin alongside low LH and low testosterone can indicate a pituitary problem that needs imaging rather than a lifestyle programme. This is one of our hard referral triggers.

What it does not show:

25-OH vitamin D

Actionable

One of the few nutrients where correcting a genuine deficiency has reasonable support. Worth measuring precisely because supplementing when you are already replete does nothing.

What it does not show:

Ferritin and iron studies

Often missed

Iron overload is a genuine and reversible cause of low testosterone, and it is easy to miss. It is also why we will never tell you to take iron without measuring first.

What it does not show:

HbA1c and fasting glucose

Actionable

Insulin resistance suppresses testosterone and tracks closely with body composition. Frequently the most useful number on the whole panel for deciding what to do first.

What it does not show:

TSH and free T4

Differential

Thyroid dysfunction produces almost exactly the symptom list people arrive with — fatigue, low mood, poor recovery, low libido. Worth excluding before assuming the cause is androgenic.

What it does not show:

Full blood count, liver and kidney function

Baseline

General safety and a baseline haematocrit. Haematocrit above 0.54 is one of our referral triggers.

What it does not show:

PSA

Over 40

If you are over 40 or have a family history. An abnormal result is a referral, not a coaching input.

What it does not show:

Reference ranges differ between laboratories; always read the range printed on your own report. Australian and New Zealand labs report testosterone in nmol/L. Educational information only, not medical advice.

The evidence

Every claim we make, and what it actually rests on.

Graded honestly, including the ones where the evidence is weak and the ones where it points against something we could have sold you. Where a finding failed to replicate, that is on the page too.

Why this page exists

In February 2026 a University of Sydney team analysed 46 high-reach testosterone posts on Instagram and TikTok, reaching 6.8 million followers between them. Not one cited any scientific evidence, and 72% had a financial interest. This page is the direct answer to that.

Sleep restriction lowers testosterone

Strong

Eight nights restricted to five hours lowered daytime testosterone by 10–15% in healthy young men. This is the largest effect of any single lifestyle factor we work with, and it is free.

What it does not show: That extending sleep beyond a normal night raises testosterone above normal. It removes a suppressor; it is not a booster.

Leproult R, Van Cauter E. JAMA 2011;305(21):2173–2174.

Losing body fat raises testosterone in men with obesity

Strong

Adipose tissue contains aromatase, which converts testosterone to oestradiol. Weight loss consistently raises total testosterone in men with obesity, and it is the largest modifiable factor in the literature.

What it does not show: That it helps lean men. If body fat is not elevated, this lever is not available to you.

Consistent across multiple randomised trials and meta-analyses.

Obstructive sleep apnoea is associated with lower testosterone

Strong

Sleep apnoea fragments the night-time architecture most testosterone release depends on. It is common, very treatable, and routinely missed — which is why our assessment screens for it.

What it does not show: That treating apnoea reliably normalises testosterone on its own. It removes an obstacle.

Luboshitzky R et al. J Clin Endocrinol Metab 2002.

Vitamin D and testosterone

Mixed — deficiency only

One year of 3,332 IU/day was associated with roughly a 25% rise in a 2011 trial. Later randomised trials in men who were already replete found no effect on testosterone at all. Our reading: correcting a genuine deficiency may help, and supplementing when you are not deficient does nothing.

What it does not show: That vitamin D raises testosterone in men with normal levels. The replication attempts were null.

Pilz S et al. Horm Metab Res 2011 (positive); Lerchbaum E et al. 2017, 2019 (null in replete men).

Zinc and testosterone

Deficiency only

Experimentally induced marginal zinc deficiency substantially reduced testosterone in young men, and repletion restored it. This is a repair mechanism, not an enhancement one.

What it does not show: That extra zinc helps anyone who is not deficient. Chronic high intake causes copper deficiency.

Prasad AS et al. Nutrition 1996;12(5):344–348.

Magnesium and testosterone

Weak, modest

A modest increase in total and free testosterone was reported with supplementation, with a larger effect in men who were exercising. We use it mostly for sleep quality, which is the better-established mechanism.

What it does not show: A large or reliable direct hormonal effect. Treat it as a sleep intervention.

Cinar V et al. Biol Trace Elem Res 2011;140(1):18–23.

Ashwagandha

Moderate

In overweight, fatigued men, 8 weeks of standardised extract was associated with roughly a 15% increase in testosterone and an 18% increase in DHEA-S. The better-evidenced herbal option, and also the one with the most real safety signal — see our safety page before taking it.

What it does not show: Long-term safety, or an effect in healthy non-fatigued men. Liver injury case reports exist.

Lopresti AL et al. Am J Mens Health 2019;13(2). Also Wankhede S et al. 2015.

Tongkat ali (Eurycoma longifolia)

Moderate

In men with late-onset hypogonadism, supplementation was associated with normalisation of testosterone in a large proportion of participants. Better trial support than most herbals in this category.

What it does not show: Product quality is the weak point, not the mechanism — heavy-metal contamination has been found in market samples.

Tambi MIBM et al. Andrologia 2012;44 Suppl 1:226–230.

Pelvic floor training improves erectile function

Strong — but not hormonal

In a randomised trial, pelvic floor muscle training outperformed lifestyle advice alone for erectile dysfunction, with a substantial proportion regaining normal function by six months. It also has support for ejaculatory control.

What it does not show: Any effect on serum testosterone. There is no good evidence it raises it, and we say so on every page where we mention it.

Dorey G et al. BJU International 2005;96(4):595–597.

Testosterone declines with age, and free testosterone faster

Strong

Longitudinal data show total testosterone falling roughly 1% per year from around age 40, with free testosterone falling faster because SHBG rises with age. This is why we track free testosterone rather than total alone.

What it does not show: That the decline is a disease requiring treatment. It is a normal trajectory.

Feldman HA et al. Massachusetts Male Aging Study. J Clin Endocrinol Metab 2002;87(2):589–598.

Tribulus terrestris does not raise testosterone

No effect

Repeatedly tested in humans and repeatedly negative, including in trained men. It remains one of the most-sold ingredients in this category, which tells you something about the category.

What it does not show: —

Multiple randomised controlled trials; consistently null for serum testosterone.

D-aspartic acid does not raise testosterone, and may lower it

No effect / harm

An early positive study drove its popularity. Subsequent trials failed to replicate it, and at higher doses testosterone decreased in resistance-trained men.

What it does not show: —

Melville GW et al. J Int Soc Sports Nutr 2015;12:15.

    How this is calculated

    This uses the Vermeulen equation (Vermeulen A, Verdonck L, Kaufman JM, J Clin Endocrinol Metab 1999;84:3666–72), the method most widely used for calculated free testosterone. It solves the law-of-mass-action equilibrium between testosterone, SHBG and albumin, using association constants of KSHBG = 1.0 × 109 L/mol and Kalb = 3.6 × 104 L/mol, with albumin taken as 69,000 g/mol and testosterone as 288.42 g/mol.

    Calculated free testosterone tracks equilibrium dialysis (the reference method) closely and is far more reliable than direct analogue free-testosterone immunoassays, which are not recommended. The reference range shown is the CDC-harmonised LC-MS/MS range for men aged 19–39 (264–916 ng/dL; 9.2–31.8 nmol/L). Your own laboratory's range takes precedence over this one.

    Conversion: 1 nmol/L = 28.84 ng/dL. So 1,000 ng/dL is 34.7 nmol/L — above the top of that reference range, not in the middle of it.

    Supplements: ceilings and cautions

    Upper levels below are for adult men and are taken from the Australian and New Zealand Nutrient Reference Values where one exists. They are ceilings, not targets. More is not better with any of these, and several are actively harmful above the line.

    Vitamin D3

    Upper level 4,000 IU/day

    Only worth taking if your 25-OH vitamin D is actually low. Trials in men who were already replete found no testosterone benefit at all. Sustained high doses can raise blood calcium, which causes nausea, constipation, confusion and kidney stones.

    Interacts with
    Thiazide diuretics and digoxin — both raise the risk of high calcium
    Stop and seek advice if
    You develop persistent nausea, unusual thirst, constipation or confusion

    Zinc

    Upper level 40 mg/day elemental

    Corrects a deficiency; it does not push a normal level higher. Long-term intake above the ceiling causes copper deficiency, which shows up as anaemia and, if it goes on, nerve damage. Take it with food — on an empty stomach it commonly causes nausea.

    Interacts with
    Tetracycline and quinolone antibiotics, and penicillamine — separate doses by at least two hours. Take away from iron and calcium
    Stop and seek advice if
    You develop unexplained fatigue, numbness or tingling on long-term use

    Magnesium

    350 mg/day elemental from supplements

    The ceiling applies to supplements only, not magnesium from food. The usual first sign of too much is loose stools. Glycinate is better tolerated than oxide, which is poorly absorbed and mostly produces the laxative effect.

    Interacts with
    Bisphosphonates and some antibiotics — separate by two hours. Take real care if you have reduced kidney function, as magnesium accumulates
    Stop and seek advice if
    You have kidney disease and have not cleared this with your doctor

    Iron

    Only on a measured deficiency

    Never supplement iron blind. Iron overload is itself a cause of low testosterone, so taking it without testing can cause the exact problem you are trying to fix. It needs a ferritin result and ideally full iron studies first.

    Interacts with
    Thyroid medication, tetracyclines, and calcium — separate by several hours
    Stop and seek advice if
    Ever taken without a ferritin result, or if you have haemochromatosis in the family

    Ashwagandha

    300–600 mg/day standardised extract

    The best-evidenced herbal here, and the one with the most real safety signal. There are published case reports of liver injury. It can also raise thyroid hormone levels and has immune-stimulating effects, which matters if you have an autoimmune condition.

    Interacts with
    Thyroid medication, immunosuppressants, sedatives and alcohol. Stop two weeks before any surgery
    Stop and seek advice if
    Your eyes or skin yellow, your urine darkens, you get pain under the right ribs, or you feel unusually exhausted — stop immediately and see a doctor

    Tongkat ali (Eurycoma longifolia)

    200–400 mg/day standardised

    Reasonable trial evidence, but limited long-term safety data. Heavy-metal contamination has been found in some products on the market, so the source genuinely matters more than the dose. Buy only from a supplier who publishes batch testing.

    Interacts with
    Not well characterised — tell your pharmacist if you take anything regularly
    Stop and seek advice if
    You cannot confirm the product has been tested for heavy metals

    Boron

    Upper level 20 mg/day

    Usually taken at 6–10 mg to lower SHBG and nudge free testosterone up. The evidence is thin but the safety margin at these doses is wide. There is no reason to go near the ceiling.

    Interacts with
    No significant interactions at these doses
    Stop and seek advice if
    You experience nausea or skin irritation

    Creatine monohydrate

    5 g/day

    Not a testosterone booster, and we do not sell it as one. It is the best-evidenced performance supplement there is, and it is included for outcomes rather than hormones. Safe in healthy people; take care with reduced kidney function.

    Interacts with
    No significant medicine interactions
    Stop and seek advice if
    You have kidney disease. Also tell anyone reading your bloods — it raises creatinine and mimics kidney impairment

    Biotin — a warning, not a recommendation

    Stop 72 hours before any blood test

    We do not sell biotin, but it is in most hair, skin and nail supplements, and people forget they are taking it. It interferes with a wide range of laboratory immunoassays, producing results that are simply wrong — including a falsely normal troponin, the test used to diagnose a heart attack.

    Interacts with
    Laboratory assays rather than medicines
    Stop and seek advice if
    You are having any blood test within 72 hours — stop it and say so at the collection centre
    The rule that covers most of it

    Correct what is measurably low, at a sensible dose, and retest. Do not take a nutrient you have not measured, do not stack five things at once so that you cannot tell what did what, and do not exceed a labelled dose because progress feels slow.

    If you take prescription medicine

    Tell your GP and your pharmacist every supplement you take, including ones you consider trivial. Pharmacists are free to talk to, they are very good at this specific question, and you do not need an appointment.

    • Blood thinners such as warfarin or the newer anticoagulants — several supplements affect bleeding risk
    • Thyroid medication — iron, calcium and ashwagandha all interfere, by different mechanisms
    • Immunosuppressants, or any autoimmune condition
    • Diabetes medication — some supplements shift blood glucose
    • Sedatives, sleeping tablets, or antidepressants — effects can stack
    • Lithium, digoxin, or diuretics — narrow margins, and mineral supplements can move them
    • Any upcoming surgery — stop supplements two weeks before and tell your anaesthetist

    If you are on opioid painkillers long term, raise it specifically. They are one of the strongest known suppressors of testosterone, it is well recognised, and it is manageable — but never stop or reduce prescribed medication on your own.

    Pelvic floor training: you can overdo this

    An over-tight pelvic floor causes its own set of problems, and they look almost identical to the problems people start training to fix. This is why kegels picked up off the internet so often make things worse: the person needed to learn to relax those muscles, and spent three months tightening them instead.

    Signs you are overdoing it

    • An ache in the perineum, or pain when sitting for a while
    • Ejaculatory control getting worse rather than better
    • Needing to wait for the stream to start, or dribbling afterwards
    • New or worsening urinary urgency
    • Pain during or after ejaculation
    • Lower back or hip tightness that arrived with the training

    Rules we will not bend on

    • Use the stop-the-stream test once, to find the muscles. Never as training — repeatedly interrupting your stream can leave the bladder emptying incompletely
    • Train relaxation as deliberately as you train contraction. Down-training is half the programme, not a cool-down
    • Do not train through pain. Pain is information, not weakness
    • Three short sessions a day beats one long one
    • If you have chronic pelvic pain, more strengthening is very unlikely to be the answer
    If symptoms get worse, not better

    Stop the training and speak to a pelvic health physiotherapist. They can assess whether your pelvic floor is weak or over-tight, which you cannot reliably work out on your own, and which determines whether the programme you are doing is helping or harming. You do not need a referral to see one in Australia or New Zealand — you can book directly.

    Around your blood test

    A badly timed or badly prepared blood test is worse than no test, because you will make decisions on it.

    • Stop biotin, and any hair, skin and nail supplement containing it, 72 hours before. Biotin interferes with a long list of assays — including the troponin test used to diagnose a heart attack
    • Go between 7am and 10am, fasting — testosterone peaks in the morning
    • Avoid heavy training for 48 hours beforehand
    • Do not test while unwell or recovering from illness. Acute illness suppresses testosterone, and the result will mislead you
    • Tell whoever reads your results that you take creatine, if you do. It raises serum creatinine and can look like kidney impairment when nothing is wrong
    • Expect to repeat the test on a second morning before anyone draws a conclusion

    Storage, and who this is not for

    • Keep iron supplements locked away from children. Iron overdose is one of the leading causes of poisoning death in young children, and a handful of adult tablets can be fatal
    • Nothing here is intended for anyone under 18
    • Nothing here is intended for women, and several ingredients are specifically unsuitable in pregnancy or breastfeeding
    • Do not share your protocol. It was built around your results, and someone else's results are different
    • Store below 25°C, out of direct sunlight, and observe the expiry date

      Your hormones are still running to their own schedule, and almost nothing written for men in their thirties applies to you. Most of what you will read online about optimising testosterone at your age is written by people selling to men twice it.

      Go here instead: Your GP, if something genuinely feels wrong.

    2. You are already on testosterone, hCG or clomiphene

      We do not prescribe or adjust hormone therapy, deliberately. That means we would be offering a second opinion without any of your history, on treatment we are not managing. That is not a useful thing for you to buy.

      Go here instead: The prescriber who already knows your case.

    3. You want testosterone prescribed

      We do not do that, and it is not an oversight. Keeping prescribing out of scope is exactly what lets us be straight with you about what the evidence supports, because we do not make more money when the answer is a drug.

      Go here instead: A GP with an interest in men's health, or an endocrinologist.

    4. Your LH is high and your testosterone is low

      That pattern points to the testes rather than to your lifestyle. Sleep, training, body composition and supplementation will not fix it, and we are not going to sell you a programme while pretending otherwise.

      Go here instead: An endocrinologist or urologist. Ask for a referral.

    5. You want a guaranteed number

      We will not give you one. For reference, 1,000 ng/dL is 34.7 nmol/L — above the top of the standard adult male reference range, not in the middle of it. Anyone promising you a specific figure in advance is either guessing or lying, and both should worry you.

      Go here instead: Nowhere. Be sceptical of whoever offered it.

    6. You have pain, a lump or swelling in a testicle

      That needs examining in person, and soon. No online service can do it, ours included, and time spent researching it is time wasted.

      Go here instead: Your GP today, not next week.

    7. You are struggling with your mood

      Low mood and low testosterone look nearly identical from the inside — flat, tired, no drive, no interest. Untangling which is which deserves proper attention, and starting with a supplement can cost you months.

      Go here instead: Your GP. It is a common overlap and a treatable one.

    8. You are not willing to get bloods done

      Then we would be guessing, and so would you. Two men with identical symptoms can have completely different panels, and the causes worth finding are only visible on one. The measurement is not a formality here, it is the product.

      Go here instead: Come back when you are ready to test.

      Tribulus has repeatedly failed to raise testosterone in human trials. D-aspartic acid looked promising in one early study and then did not replicate — at higher doses it has been shown to lower testosterone. We do not stock either, and we are not going to start.

    10. You want this to be quick

      The hormone lever runs 8 to 16 weeks and needs a retest to confirm anything actually moved. If that sounds slow, it is because it is real. The things that work quickly in this category are the things that are not true.
